About me

I studied Mechanical Engineering (B.Sc.) and Aerospace Engineering (M.Sc.) at the University of Stuttgart. In my master's programme I focused on mathematical and physical modelling as well as spaceflight and space utilisation.

During my master's thesis and later as a research associate at the German Aerospace Center (DLR), I worked on optical measurement techniques, high-pressure combustion facilities and data-driven analysis with Python. In parallel, I have studied nuclear energy technologies, reactor physics and nuclear materials science in depth.

Since 2026 I have been working as a test engineer at RECARO Aircraft Seating, where I am responsible for dynamic crash tests of aircraft seats.

I also tutor school students in STEM subjects.

My interests

Aerospace & propulsion

Propulsion systems, flow dynamics and energy systems in aerospace applications. Innovative and alternative propulsion concepts are of particular interest to me.

Nuclear energy: fission & fusion

Reactor physics, neutronics, Generation IV concepts and material behaviour under irradiation – as independent study alongside university and work. Plus plasma physics, reactor concepts and the system-level feasibility of future fusion power plants.

Data-driven models & AI

Using Python and numerical methods to link experimental measurement data with physical models. I am currently expanding my skills by learning C++ for high-performance, efficient implementations.

Theoretical & applied physics

Fundamentals of quantum mechanics and spectroscopy from my master's programme, complemented by in-depth self-study of the mathematics behind physical concepts. Further interests include relativity and particle accelerators.

Astronomy

Practical astronomical observation as part of a university examination, including the analysis of an exoplanet transit. In the process I gained experience in remotely operating a telescope at the Sierra Remote Observatory in California.

Beyond that

Financial mathematics: modelling and valuing derivatives, for example with the Black–Scholes approach. Neuroscience: how biological learning principles can be transferred to technical systems. Philosophy: from Plato and Aristotle to Schopenhauer and Nietzsche – the origin of my fascination with science and mathematics.
